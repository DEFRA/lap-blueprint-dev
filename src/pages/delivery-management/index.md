# Delivery Management

## Product delivery life cycle

LAP projects follow a linear, waterfall product delivery life cycle made up of eight sequential stages. The stages run in order, with the outputs of each forming the inputs to the next, providing a consistent structure for planning, governance and assurance across delivery. The nine stages are as follows:

<details><summary>

### 1. Start-up and feasibility

</summary>

During the Start-up and Feasibility phase, establish the foundations for successful project delivery and prepare for Stage Gate 1.

This includes setting up project governance, completing key assessments such as the Risk Potential Assessment (RPA), Business Criticality Assessment, accessibility, security, sustainability, equality and data protection reviews, and creating the initial business case, budget plan, financial forecast and Integrated Assurance and Approvals Plan (IAAP).

You should also define project roles and responsibilities, set up your project plan and reporting in Project Online, engage stakeholders, and ensure compliance with Defra standards for architecture, security, accessibility, user research and software development. Throughout the phase, maintain project documentation, financial management, reporting and RAID logs.

</details>
<details><summary>

### 2. Initiation and scoping

</summary>

During the Initiation and Scoping phase, the project vision, scope and requirements are refined to ensure there is a clear and agreed delivery approach.

The Project Manager should confirm service criticality through Service Tiering, develop detailed business and user requirements, review project scope and delivery plans, and establish the controls needed to support procurement, delivery and benefits realisation.

Key artefacts produced during this phase include the requirements document, benefits realisation plan, communications plan, security plan and any required supplier work orders (PIRs).

Ongoing activities include stakeholder engagement, financial management, reporting, RAID management, document control, Whole Life Cost (WLC) planning and maintaining Secure-by-Design assurance.

</details>
<details><summary>

### 3. Define/Discovery

</summary>

During the Define phase, the project scope, requirements, solution approach and business justification are refined to establish a clear and approved basis for delivery.

Project teams should review and update governance arrangements through the Integrated Approvals and Assurance Plan (IAAP), complete key assurance activities including architecture approval, accessibility, security, sustainability, equality and data protection assessments, and develop detailed deliverables such as product descriptions and the Full Business Case.

This phase also includes progressing Secure-by-Design activities, obtaining Whole Life Cost (WLC) approval, engaging operational and technical teams to prepare future service environments, and maintaining robust financial, stakeholder, reporting and RAID management processes.

</details>
<details><summary>

### 4. Design

</summary>

During the Design phase, project requirements are translated into detailed solution designs, technical specifications and service blueprints that will inform delivery and implementation.

The project team should work closely with architecture, security and operational readiness teams to mature the solution design, establish service support requirements and prepare for future live service operations.

Key activities include progressing service readiness through GRIP and Service Design Review Board (SDRB) engagement, updating data protection, security and communications plans, managing supplier work orders where required, and continuing Secure-by-Design assurance.

Ongoing governance includes maintaining financial controls, stakeholder engagement, project reporting, RAID management, documentation and Whole Life Cost (WLC) planning.

The phase concludes when the solution design has been agreed, operational readiness activities are underway, and the project is prepared to progress into build and implementation activities.

</details>
<details><summary>

### 5. Build

</summary>

During the Build phase, the project team develops and configures the solution in line with the approved designs, requirements and architecture.

This includes establishing and managing technical environments, implementing code and infrastructure changes, planning and preparing testing activities, and progressing operational readiness for future deployment.

Governance activities during this phase focus on maintaining security, privacy and commercial controls through ongoing Secure-by-Design assurance, DPIA updates, supplier work orders where required, and change management processes for technical environments.

The project team should continue regular financial management, stakeholder engagement, delivery reporting, RAID management, documentation and Whole Life Cost (WLC) forecasting to ensure the project remains on track.

The phase concludes when the solution has been built, testing preparations are complete, and the project is ready to transition into formal testing and deployment activities.

</details>
<details><summary>

### 6. Test

</summary>

During the Test phase, the project team validates that the solution meets business, user, security, accessibility and operational requirements before release.

Testing activities should be executed in line with the agreed Test Plan and include functional, integration, accessibility, security and user acceptance testing, with defects managed and resolved through to completion.

The project team should continue to progress service readiness activities, operational support arrangements and technical environment governance, while maintaining Secure-by-Design assurance, DPIA updates, IT Health Checks, Whole Life Cost (WLC) approvals and any required supplier work orders.

Key outputs include the Test Completion Report, service readiness certification, accessibility assurance and updated governance documentation such as the IAAP. Ongoing financial management, stakeholder engagement, reporting, RAID management and documentation controls must continue throughout the phase.

The phase concludes with successful confirmation that testing objectives have been met, and readiness to transition the service into implementation and live operation.

</details>
<details><summary>

### 7. Implementation

</summary>

During the Implementation phase, the project transitions from delivery into live operation, ensuring that the service, supporting teams and governance arrangements are prepared for ongoing management and support.

The focus is on completing operational handover activities, embedding service management processes, validating ongoing security and data protection requirements, and preparing the project for closure.

Key activities include updating communications and transition plans, maintaining Secure-by-Design assurance, reviewing service performance and improvement opportunities, updating the Risk Potential Assessment (RPA), managing supplier arrangements where required, and ensuring all project documentation is complete and ready for handover.

Ongoing financial management, stakeholder engagement, project reporting, RAID management and business case maintenance continue throughout the phase.

The phase concludes when the service is operating successfully, ownership has transitioned to the appropriate live service teams, and the project is ready to enter formal project closure.

</details>
<details><summary>

### 8. Closure

</summary>

During the Closure phase, the project formally concludes once the objectives and outcomes defined in the business case have been delivered and transitioned into business-as-usual support.

The focus is on evidencing the benefits achieved, confirming financial and governance activities are complete, documenting lessons learned, and ensuring any remaining risks, issues or actions have clear ownership beyond project closure.

Key activities include finalising financial records, updating the business case and Critical Success Factors (CSFs), reviewing Secure-by-Design outcomes, archiving project documentation, transferring Whole Life Cost (WLC) ownership to the Business Service Owner, and producing the appropriate closure report based on project spend.

Following closure, responsibility for ongoing service operation, maintenance and improvement transfers to the appropriate business-as-usual service team.

</details>
